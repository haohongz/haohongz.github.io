2026 – present

Uncertainty quantification for tabular foundation models

With Prof. Zijian Guo (Zhejiang University) and Prof. Molei Liu (Peking University)

TabPFN-type models give accurate predictions on small tables, but they come with no confidence interval for the conditional mean. We build intervals that reach their nominal coverage under repeated sampling. I ran the Monte Carlo study, with five data-generating processes and 100 runs each, and built the experiment pipeline on a SLURM GPU cluster. In these simulations the naive baselines covered the truth 33% to 68% of the time at a 95% nominal level; the proposed intervals stay close to 95%.

Manuscript: Y. Shi, H. Zheng, M. Liu, Z. Guo. Context-Uncertainty Quantification for Tabular Foundation Models. 2026.

2026

Knowledge-guided TabPFN

With Prof. Molei Liu (Peking University)

A tabular foundation model does not know what its columns mean. We inject an external knowledge graph (Hetionet, with TransE embeddings) into a frozen TabPFN or TabICL through small trainable gates, for prediction with far more features than samples. On simulated data with interaction structure, injecting the graph at the attention level raised accuracy from about 50% to 73%. I contributed the adapter design, ran the layer-wise experiments, and reviewed more than 40 related papers to position the method.

Synthetic survival data for electronic health records

With Prof. Kevin He, Kidney Epidemiology and Cost Center, University of Michigan

Synthetic patient records are useful only if they keep the time-to-event structure of the real data. Our group is building a benchmark for generators of synthetic survival data. I reproduced GAN-based generators such as SurvivalGAN under one interface so they can be compared directly, and I work on the evaluation side: how closely a synthetic cohort matches the real one in its survival distribution and its censoring pattern.
